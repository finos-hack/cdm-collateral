## Maintainer

Open items for row 15 (recall). I recommend "a" on all of them.

1. **The notice record.** a: A cdm-ext `GmslaRecallNotice` (the loan, the quantity recalled, the notice date, the
   intended settlement date, the trade hash it recalls), rebuilt and checked by the borrower's service, its hash anchored
   on-chain. b: Off-chain only, in the services.
2. **On the contract.** a: A new `SecuritiesLoan.recall(loanId, quantity, settlementDate, noticeHash)` (lender only)
   records one pending recall per loan; the borrower settles through the existing `instructReturn` path; a settled
   return of at least the recalled quantity clears the recall. b: The lender's recall counts as its half of the return
   instruction and the borrower's instruction settles it (both would then build the return document on the notice date,
   not the settlement date). Either is a contract change and a new deployment.
3. **Intended settlement date.** a: The notice date plus 2 business days (weekends skipped), the standard settlement
   cycle. b: The lender enters the date.
4. **CDM's `RecallProvision` on the trade.** a: Book new loans with `RecallProvision` (`isRecallable`,
   `intendedSettlementDate` 2 business days after the notice), and the notice takes its date from it. b: Leave the trade
   as it is.
5. **Partial recall.** a: The lender recalls all or part of the quantity outstanding. b: All only.
6. **The borrower misses the date.** a: Both services and the console show the recall overdue once the business date
   passes it; no remedy here (buy-in and default are row 19). b: Also let the lender buy in now.
7. **Collateral.** a: Unchanged until the return, which redelivers collateral as in row 13 (`GmslaReturnCollateral`);
   row 14's marking counts the full loan until then. b: Release collateral at the notice.
8. **Withdrawal.** a: The lender can withdraw a recall before the return settles; anchored with its own hash. b: A
   recall cannot be withdrawn.
9. **Live spec `flow-gmsla-recall`.** a: A lends 100,000 tMMF to B against tUSD; A recalls 40,000 in the console; B
   sees the due date and returns 40,000, A agrees, the recall clears; A recalls the rest, the business date moves past
   the due date and it shows overdue; B returns the rest and the loan is returned; a recall that is withdrawn moves
   nothing; every balance ends where it started. b: A single full recall and return.
