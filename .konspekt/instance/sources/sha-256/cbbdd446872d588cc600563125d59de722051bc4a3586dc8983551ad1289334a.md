## Design options

These are for `task-triparty-revaluation`. Option (a) is the one I recommend in each case.

1. **Required value:** (a) Purchase Price plus the Price Differential accrued to the revaluation date; (b) Purchase Price only, as at trade start.
2. **Threshold:** (a) none: any shortfall is topped up and any excess released, in whole units; (b) a minimum transfer amount added to `TripartyTerms`, which needs a redeploy.
3. **Top-up source:** (a) the giver's free account in the giver's ranking, within the limits, using the allocation's logic on the shortfall; (b) only assets already allocated.
4. **Release order:** (a) lowest-ranked asset first, never below the required value; (b) pro rata.
5. **Free account cannot cover the shortfall:** (a) nothing moves, and the revaluation is recorded as uncovered; (b) top up what is available and record the remainder.
6. **Limit breached by a price move alone:** (a) record it and leave it; substitution (row 23) fixes it; (b) the revaluation rebalances it.
7. **CDM record:** (a) a cdm-ext `TripartyRevaluation` document, a valuation event, and a terms-change event that keeps the Purchase Date, plus a new cdm-ext qualifier that separates a top-up or release from a substitution; (b) the valuation event only, with the positions tracked outside the trade.
8. **Verification:** (a) the giver and the receiver recompute from the feed prices and the free account, as at trade start.
9. **Contract:** (a) `TripartySettlement.revalue`, callable by the agent only, moves units between the free and repo accounts in one transaction, needing a new deployment; (b) the receiver affirms each move.
10. **Live spec:** (a) `flow-triparty-revaluation`: the price drops and the agent tops up, then the price rises and the agent releases.
