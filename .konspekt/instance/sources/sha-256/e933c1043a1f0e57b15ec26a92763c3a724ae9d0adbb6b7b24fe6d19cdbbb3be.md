## Design options

These are for `task-triparty-substitution`. Option (a) is the one I recommend in each case.

1. **Request:** (a) the giver names an asset and the units it wants back; (b) the giver names an asset and gets all of it back.
2. **Replacement:** (a) from the giver's free account in the giver's ranking, without the returned asset, within the limits, until the holding again covers the Repurchase Price accrued to the agent's date; (b) at least equal value to the returned units, whatever the cover.
3. **Not enough free collateral:** (a) the agent declines, nothing moves, and the decline is recorded; (b) the agent returns what it can cover.
4. **Breaches left by revaluation:** (a) the agent can also start a substitution itself to swap out the excess of an asset over its limit; (b) breaches stay until the giver asks.
5. **Receiver:** (a) no affirmation, consistent with revaluation; (b) the receiver affirms each swap.
6. **On-chain steps:** (a) the giver records its request hash, then the agent substitutes or declines in a second transaction, with one pending request per repo; (b) the request goes off-chain to the agent's service, and only the swap is on-chain.
7. **CDM record:** (a) a cdm-ext `TripartySubstitution` document with the request, the agent's computation, the terms change keeping the Purchase Date, and CDM workflow steps for the request and its execution; (b) the same without the workflow steps.
8. **Verification:** (a) the giver and receiver recompute it from the chain at the block before, as for revaluation.
9. **Contract:** (a) `requestSubstitution` (giver), and `substitute` and `declineSubstitution` (agent only, where `substitute` reuses the revaluation's swap of holdings), needing a new deployment.
10. **Live spec:** (a) `flow-triparty-substitution`: B asks for 1,000 tAGY back and the agent swaps in 998 tUST; then B asks for more than its free tUST covers and the agent declines.
