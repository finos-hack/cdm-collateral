# ADR (proposed): document provenance as cdm-ext types, exported as PROV-O; token-level anchors in LedgerAnchor; tUST pilot without credentials

For [[task-document-provenance]] (gap G41, finding N2), under [[goal-tokenized-collateral]]. The project owner chose an
existing standard mapped to CDM for interoperability: W3C PROV-O for the links, W3C Verifiable Credentials for record
keepers' signed statements (2026-11-21). This ADR checks that choice against the alternatives and proposes an option for
each of the four open questions. Each recommendation waits for the project owner's choice.

## Standards checked (2026-11-22)

| Standard | What it covers | Fit for this task |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|
| W3C PROV-O (Recommendation, 2013) | A general vocabulary of entities, activities and agents and the relations between them (derived from, generated by, attributed to, used); meant to be specialised per domain | The only W3C standard for exchanging provenance. Fits the links between parties, assets, tokens and documents. Has no integrity or signatures, no financial terms, and little use in financial services |
| W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 (Recommendations, May 2025: data model, Data Integrity, ECDSA and EdDSA cryptosuites, JOSE and COSE, status list) | Signed, revocable statements by an issuer about a subject | Fits a record keeper's statement that a link holds (the register shows this holding; this document is the executed version). Complements PROV-O |
| ISO 17442-3:2024, verifiable LEI (vLEI; ACDC credentials, KERI keys) | Organisational identity and the roles of people acting for it, chained to GLEIF as root of trust | The strongest standard for the party leg (who the issuer, record keeper or lender is). A different credential stack from W3C VC; not needed for a prototype with fixture parties |
| FIBO (EDM Council, standardised by OMG; OWL) | Financial concepts: legal entities, contracts, securities | Domain classes to specialise PROV-O with (a security, a legal entity, a contract), so the export is readable by FIBO users. CDM stays the system of record |
| ERC-3643 with ONCHAINID (final ERC) | On-chain identity claims that decide who may hold and receive a permissioned token | Eligibility of holders, not provenance of documents. Relevant to the token contract; out of this task's scope |
| Ethereum Attestation Service (EAS); EEA Shibui (EAS with ERC-3643) | A general on-chain or off-chain attestation registry for EVM chains, with schemas, revocation and expiry | An alternative way to anchor statements on the ledger, in place of the prototype's own anchors. Covered under question 2 |

**Assessment.** PROV-O is the right vocabulary for the links: it is a stable W3C Recommendation, built to be
specialised, and nothing newer covers the same ground. It does not give integrity, signatures or financial meaning on
its own, so it works with three other pieces: document hashes anchored on the ledger (integrity), Verifiable
Credentials (signed statements) and FIBO classes (financial meaning). vLEI is the better long-term source of party
identity than self-issued credentials, but adds a second credential stack; it waits until real parties are involved.

## Question 1: representation

- **A (recommended).** `cdm-ext` types for the links (`ProvenanceLink`: subject, relation, object, document reference with
  content hash, record keeper, valid from, superseded by) with Rune conditions, and a Java exporter that writes them as
  PROV-O JSON-LD, with FIBO classes where one fits. Follows [[concept-asr-cdm-data-layer]]: CDM JSON first, checks in
  Rune.
- **B.** PROV-O JSON-LD as the record; CDM documents refer to it by hash. Less work in `cdm-ext`, but the checks move
  out of Rune and the record is no longer CDM JSON.

## Question 2: where token-level links are anchored

Agreements between two parties are already anchored in `CollateralControl` (per-pair CSA and master-agreement chains)
and `TripartySettlement`. Token terms, valuations, registry entries and the control agreement belong to the token.

- **A (recommended).** New `AnchoredRecordEnum` kinds (`AssetDocument`, `Valuation`, `RegistryEntry`) in `LedgerAnchor`,
  keyed by token, agreed with [[task-substitution-ledger-anchors]], which builds anchors in the same code. Reuses the
  existing anchor status and checks.
- **B.** A separate provenance contract keyed by token, with a chain per token like the per-pair chains.
- **C.** EAS: one schema per link type, attested on the Besu network. Uses a widely deployed contract and gives
  revocation and expiry, but adds a dependency and departs from the prototype's own anchors.

## Question 3: the tUST pilot's documents

tUST (ISIN US912810TM60, DTI QX4V2W9N3) is a Bearer token with no documents in the deployment. Proposed fixture links:
token to ISIN (the security); ISIN to issuer (US Treasury); token to its token terms (a fixture document); token to its
tokenizer and custodian (a fixture party); and a pledge of tUST under the CSA, through the existing per-pair chain.
Valuation links come from the oracle's prices.

## Question 4: Verifiable Credentials in the pilot

- **A (recommended).** Not in the pilot: PROV links and anchored hashes only. Credentials come with tMMF, where a record
  keeper's statement about its register matters.
- **B.** In the pilot, signed with the parties' Besu keys. Adds key and DID handling to the smallest case.

## Consequences

The tUST pilot is built and tested in `cdm-ext` and the collateral service; the done criterion in
[[task-document-provenance]] is unchanged. The SME questions in [[task-sme-document-provenance]] still decide which
links a court or auditor would accept; they do not block the pilot.
